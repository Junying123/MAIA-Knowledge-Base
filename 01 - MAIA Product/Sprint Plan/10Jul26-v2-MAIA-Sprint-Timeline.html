Delivery engine — operating principles (from the SOP · what the sprint calibrates)

Management = checkers, not doers

Any product member picks up any account and knows the next step from the SOP — without asking. Mgmt are checkers & stand-ins; they step in only when things derail.

Three lenses before kickoff

Scope-lock (what we owe) · VoC (why they bought) · end-user process + UAT cases (how we prove it). Locked before the kickoff, not after.

UAT checklist upfront

Run the test cases past the client before development — not authored 1–2 days before UAT. This is the Fixguru-loss fix.

Per-stage discipline

Every stage carries owner · SLA date · definition-of-done · "if it slips" trigger. Dates are benchmarks that generate signals — investigate the process, don't blame the person.

AI accelerates, doesn't replace

The PM owns and iterates the AI output and embodies the three lenses. Generate-and-assume-done is the failure mode.

Sales hands over raw materials only

Signed proposal + transcripts. Product self-generates the narrative + scope-lock — the product lens goes deeper than sales.

Trust + Throughput

Product owns throughput; dev owns trust = accuracy (UAT/UX) + reliability (deploys don't break what was stable). Dev SOP owned by Jermaine.

Board numbers + the 57-min check

< 8 deployments in flight · > 10 go-lives / month. Every go-live gets the 57-min QC: sample 20 random customers, tally across both systems — one or two wrong = the whole thing is wrong.

Definitions — lock these so status can't drift

Gap

A delta preventing UAT acceptance. Triage tags type: (a) not-built · (b) buggy · (c) client-unconfirmed.

Clarity

Gaps identified + clarification questions dispatched — NOT answers received.

Done

Client-signed acceptance. Not "tech ticket closed." Sign-off authority mapped per account.
