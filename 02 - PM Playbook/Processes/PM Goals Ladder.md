# PM Goals Ladder

This document connects the PM Personal Operating System to a practical goals hierarchy:

`yearly direction -> 90-day outcomes -> monthly focus -> weekly goals -> daily execution`

Use this as the top-level goals map for your working style, decision quality, account ownership, AI leverage, and improvement loops.

## Yearly Goals

Yearly goals define the **operating identity** you are building — the PM you want to be by end of year. These are not time-boxed tasks. They describe permanent capabilities you are growing toward. The 90-day goals below are the specific outcomes you commit to achieving this cycle in service of these yearly goals.

### 1. Become consistently clear on account and workstream priorities

**Success looks like**
- You can explain the top priority, main risk, and next step for each key account quickly.
- Less work gets rediscovered late.
- You spend more time on meaningful movement and less on reactive cleanup.

**Evidence**
- Account tracker is consistently maintained.
- Weekly reviews show clear priority judgment.
- Missed follow-ups trend downward.

**Supported by**

- [[02 - PM Playbook/Templates/[Template] Weekly Operating Review]]

### 2. Become trusted for sharper judgment, earlier escalation, and better communication

**Success looks like**
- Your updates are concise, structured, and useful.
- Risks are raised earlier.
- Dev, management, and clients get clearer framing and next steps from you.

**Evidence**
- Important updates increasingly follow outcome, risk, recommendation, next step.
- Blockers are raised early more often than late.
- Weekly reviews show fewer cases of reactive escalation.

**Supported by**
- [[02 - PM Playbook/Templates/[Template] Daily Operating Plan]]
- [[02 - PM Playbook/Templates/[Template] Weekly Operating Review]]
- [[02 - PM Playbook/Templates/[Template] KISS Retrospective]]

### 3. Build a repeatable AI-assisted workflow that improves quality without reducing ownership

**Success looks like**
- AI is used intentionally on high-stakes, ambiguous, or multi-dependency work.
- AI helps catch weak logic, unclear messaging, or hidden risks before others do.
- You keep final judgment instead of outsourcing it.

**Evidence**
- High-stakes tasks reviewed by AI before execution.
- Useful prompts are reused.
- Weekly review shows where AI helped and where it created noise.

**Success looks like**
- The daily and weekly cadence becomes normal, not forced.
- You feel less lost on what to do next.
- Retros produce real changes instead of vague lessons.

**Evidence**
- Daily plans used consistently.
- Weekly reviews completed.
- KISS and GRAI retros produce follow-through changes.

## Current 90-Day Goals (2026-06-01 → 2026-08-29)

### Goal 1. Run the daily and weekly operating cadence consistently

**Success looks like**
- Daily planning happens on most workdays.
- Weekly review happens every week.
- Your work feels more directed and less reactive.

**Milestones / proof points**
- Daily plans completed at least 4 times per week for 2 consecutive weeks.
- Weekly review completed 2 weeks in a row.
- End-of-day close and KISS completed on most planning days.
- You can point to one concrete priority decision per week that improved because of the cadence.

**Evidence source**
- Daily Operating Plan
- Weekly Operating Review
- KISS Retrospective

### Goal 2. Maintain account visibility and priority control

**Success looks like**
- Each key account has a current top priority, blocker, next follow-up, and next milestone.
- You can tell what is strategic, operational, or maintenance work.

**Milestones / proof points**
- Account dashboard maintained for all active accounts or workstreams.
- Every weekly review identifies top priority and biggest risk per active account.
- At least one low-value item is deprioritised each week because the goal ladder made it obvious.
- Fewer surprises or rediscovered follow-ups.

**Evidence source**
- Account Ownership Dashboard
- Weekly Operating Review

### Goal 3. Improve quality of briefs, updates, and escalations

**Success looks like**
- Communication gets sharper by audience.
- Dev briefs cause less ambiguity.
- Management updates are more recommendation-led.

**Milestones / proof points**
- Important updates include outcome, risk, recommendation, and next step.
- At least one dev/management/client message per week is reviewed through the AI layer.
- Weekly review records fewer cases of vague or late communication.
- One meaningful example per month where better framing prevented confusion.

**Evidence source**
- Daily Operating Plan
- Weekly Operating Review
- KISS Retrospective

### Goal 4. Use AI intentionally across planning, prioritisation, communication, and retros

**Success looks like**
- AI is used where it adds leverage, not everywhere.
- You know which prompts are reliably useful.
- AI helps sharpen judgment rather than create noise.

**Milestones / proof points**
- AI used on high-stakes work with accepted and rejected advice recorded.
- Weekly review captures where AI saved time, caught risk, or created overthinking.
- One reusable prompt or workflow is identified each week.
- At least one KISS or GRAI retro per cycle meaningfully improved by AI critique.

**Evidence source**
- Daily Operating Plan
- Weekly Operating Review
- KISS Retrospective
- GRAI Retrospective

### Goal 5. Build evidence through metrics and retros that the system is working

**Success looks like**
- You can point to real behavior change, not only good intentions.
- Metrics and retros feed directly into the next cycle of improvement.

**Milestones / proof points**
- Weekly metrics are filled consistently.
- One monthly pattern is identified and acted on.
- One major GRAI retro is completed during the 90-day cycle.

## Monthly Focus

Use one monthly focus at a time. Rotate the emphasis, but keep the yearly and 90-day goals stable through the cycle.

### Month 1: Consistency (June 2026)

**Focus**
- Make the daily and weekly cadence real and repeatable.

**Proof points**
- Daily plan used on most workdays.
- Weekly review completed every week.
- Account dashboard kept current.
- KISS used consistently enough to spot patterns.

### Month 2: Prioritisation and escalation (July 2026)

**Focus**
- Improve what gets attention first and what gets raised earlier.

**Proof points**
- Priority scoring used before deep work on key items.
- Earlier escalation on blockers and dependencies.
- At least one low-value task or noise stream intentionally cut.
- Weekly review shows better tradeoff decisions.

### Month 3: Communication and AI leverage (August 2026)

**Focus**
- Sharpen messaging by audience and use AI more intentionally.

**Proof points**
- Important updates get AI review when stakes are high.
- One reused prompt or workflow per week.
- Better briefs and clearer escalations visible in weekly evidence.
- One GRAI retro uses AI to sharpen insight, not just wording.

## Weekly Goals

Each week should aim to:
- complete the weekly review
- keep the account tracker current
- protect the top priorities
- catch and escalate blockers earlier

**Success looks like**
- The week closes with clear evidence of what moved, what slipped, and what changes next.

**Evidence**
- Weekly Operating Review completed

## Daily Goals

Each day should aim to:
- define top 3 outcomes
- score priority before deep work
- run at least one perspective check on high-stakes work
- use AI on high-stakes or ambiguous items
- complete end-of-day close and KISS

**Success looks like**
- You know what matters most, what to do next, and what must not be missed.

**Evidence**
- Daily Operating Plan completed
- AI critique captured where relevant
- Daily KISS completed

## Goal Selection Rule

When work appears urgent, ask:
- Which weekly goal does this support?
- Which 90-day goal does this support?
- Does this move a yearly operating goal, or is it maintenance/noise?

**Tiebreaker:** If it supports none of the above, apply one of these actions — do not leave it in limbo:
- **Defer:** Low urgency, low risk. Schedule it or batch it with admin time.
- **Kill:** No clear owner, no clear outcome, no stakeholder who would notice if it disappeared.
- **Escalate:** Someone else assigned it and you're unsure if it belongs to you — clarify ownership before doing work.

### Examples

- **Defer**
  - Nice-to-have note cleanup that does not affect a client, deadline, or blocker this week.
- **Kill**
  - Extra analysis or formatting that makes the work look polished but does not change any decision, delivery step, or stakeholder outcome.
- **Escalate**
  - A cross-team request with unclear ownership, or a task that may impact timeline or scope but should not be decided alone.
