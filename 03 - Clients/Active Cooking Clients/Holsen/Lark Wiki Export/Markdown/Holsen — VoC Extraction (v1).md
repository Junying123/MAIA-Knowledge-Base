**Coverage verdict: proceed-with-caveats.**

What this corpus licenses: strong, load-bearing evidence for **one named customer-side actor** --- Tam Ze Xin (or whoever operates the shared holsenlab@gmail.com account, most likely him) --- across pricing, tax, C1/C3 compliance, and batch allocation. One additional named actor, Chin Zhao Heng (the boss), appears clearly in a single early transcript (Feb 10) and gives the clearest, cleanest explanation of the C1/C3/K1/batch model in the whole corpus.

What it does not license: **direct voice from Wong Shui Fern (Finance), Noor Aili Nafiah (Logistics), Intan Atikah (Procurement), Murugesu (Production), or Ong Siow Chui (System Admin).** Every requirement attributed to these roles in the vault (e.g. \"Aili approves proforma,\" \"Miss Wong generates e-Invoice\") comes from Tam or Chin describing their colleagues\' workflow secondhand, or from vendor-authored feature-request docs --- never from those people\'s own mouths in this corpus. Their operational pain, if any, is entirely unrepresented. Sales-side voice (Ng Tze Chien) is also entirely absent.

The corpus also leans heavily on Fireflies/Granola transcripts with **severely degraded transcription quality** --- long stretches of multilingual noise, garbled fragments, and at least one file (2026-04-27) that is functionally unusable. Where the transcript is bad but the topic and speaker role are still identifiable from context, content is tagged BELIEVED, not CONFIRMED, even when the underlying signal looks solid. Three internal dev-team Granola sessions (04-16, 04-30, 05-04) contain zero customer voice --- pure Mindhive engineering discussion about certificate data models --- and are excluded outright.

Six additional sources listed in the task brief were not opened in this pass due to time-boxing (05-08, 05-11 ×2, 05-12 ×2, \"Holsen v3 prep work,\" \"Meeting - Holsen - MH MAIA Setup and testing Mar 18,\" \"Feature Requests - 18 March,\" \"SOW Feature Checklist,\" \"User Guide,\" and the two Lark vendor docs). None of the unopened sources are flagged elsewhere in the vault as containing primary customer voice beyond what the two deeply-read UAT sessions (05-15, 05-22) already cover, but this is stated, not verified --- treat the \"what we do not know\" table below as the honest boundary of this run, and re-run against the unopened sources before treating this as final.

**INFERENCE \[BELIEVED, anchors: VOC-021, VOC-022, VOC-023\]** --- Holsen\'s mental model of C1 vs. C3 is not \"two similar compliance certificates\" --- it\'s \"a standing relationship I trust once verified\" (C1) versus \"a one-time legal custody arrangement with a specific counterparty\" (C3). **INFERENCE \[BELIEVED, anchors: VOC-015, VOC-016, VOC-017, VOC-033\]** --- Holsen\'s pricing model is not \"one price list\" but two entirely different pricing philosophies coexisting on one item master: fixed, negotiated, per-customer pricing for goods they manufacture, and floating, commodity-tracked pricing for goods they trade. **INFERENCE \[CONFIRMED, anchors: VOC-025, VOC-026\]** --- Holsen frames compliance not as a checkbox but as an active differentiator from industry norms they consider looser than their own practice. **Bottom Line**

  --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Holsen isn\'t asking MAIA to invent a new compliance system. They already have one --- it lives in Excel, in a shared phone number\'s memory, and in \"creative bookkeeping\" when the paperwork and the physical stock stop matching. What they need from MAIA is for the system to be at least as good as that informal process at knowing which portion of a batch belongs to whom, without becoming a new source of audit risk in the process. Everything else in this corpus --- pricing defaults, tax rates, permission scopes, PSO forms --- the customer either tested calmly and moved on, or explicitly said could wait. Only one thing produced a spontaneous, detailed, numbers-heavy, unprompted explanation and an admission of \"we don\'t know how to solve this\" after \"so many years\" --- batch-to-customer stock allocation under C1/C3 exemption.

  --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The single product mistake most likely to sink this account isn\'t a missing feature --- it\'s building batch allocation as a rigid lock instead of the flexible, override-capable reservation the customer actually described (VOC-006). A hard lock that blocks the boss\'s legitimate \"pull from this customer\'s stock, top it back up later\" pattern would get worked around the same way UBS\'s rigid CSV format did --- quietly, outside the system, defeating the entire point of adopting MAIA for compliance in the first place.
