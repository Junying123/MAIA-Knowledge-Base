JDX Gift and Food Sdn. Bhd. is a Kuala Lumpur-based distributor and retailer of premium Chinese teas, specialty foods, and seasonal gift hampers. The company is the official authorised distributor of 大益 (DaYi / TaeTea) — one of China's most recognised Pu'er tea brands — and runs a multi-channel business serving corporate buyers, hypermarket chains, and individual consumers primarily during peak gifting seasons.

## 5. Customer Problems

- **Peak-season volume spikes with no scalable infrastructure:** Corporate hamper orders surge to hundreds per day during CNY and Hari Raya, then drop to near zero off-season. JDX cannot maintain large permanent headcount to match peak demand and must hire and train temporary staff for each festival cycle, with all the coordination overhead that entails.

- **Corporate order customisation complexity:** Each hamper order can include specific ribbon colours, greeting card wording, item substitutions (e.g. mushroom → pineapple tart), price tag preferences, and delivery timing — all captured in free-text remarks fields across physical documents and WhatsApp messages. Errors in passing these details through to warehouse and delivery are common.

- **Multi-address delivery coordination:** A single corporate order often means dozens of separate delivery destinations. Coordinating drivers, tracking proof-of-delivery photos, following up on failed or refused deliveries, and managing outstation 3PL parcels is done manually across WhatsApp groups and spreadsheets.

- **Payment matching before delivery release:** JDX's policy requires payment confirmation before goods are released. Corporate customers send payment advice slips to a dedicated WhatsApp group; coordinators manually match each slip to the corresponding pro forma and only then authorise the delivery order. During peak this creates a bottleneck and overnight stand-by requirements.

- **Consignment sell-through reconciliation:** At Giant and AEON kiosks, promoters file daily stock reports via WhatsApp. Ops staff must read, verify, and challenge reports — then decide on top-up quantities. Monthly billing goes through the hypermarket's own B2B portal and involves commission and display charge deductions, all tracked outside any central system.

- **Inventory forecasting difficulty:** JDX forecasts procurement "by feeling" for seasonal peaks, with around 70% accuracy. Stock management runs on Excel and manual checks; the SQL system is used for accounting codes only. Over- and under-ordering are recurring risks, and substitutions with customer consent are common when items run short.

## 6. Solutions Provided

- **Authorised DaYi distribution:** As the official Malaysian distributor of 大益 (DaYi), JDX gives customers access to authenticated, traceable Pu'er teas sourced directly from China — addressing the market problem of counterfeit or grey-market premium teas.

- **End-to-end seasonal hamper fulfilment:** JDX handles corporate hamper orders from customisation and packing through to multi-address delivery, including own-fleet Klang Valley drops and outstation 3PL coordination. Customers outsource the entire gifting logistics to one supplier.

- **Flexible customisation per order:** Ribbon colours, greeting card wording, item swaps, packaging formats, and delivery instructions are all accommodated per order — serving corporate buyers whose recipients have varied preferences and internal gift-giving policies.

- **Multi-channel retail access:** Customers can reach JDX through retail stores, hypermarket kiosks (Giant/AEON), direct WhatsApp/Facebook inquiries, and the website — giving buyers flexibility in how they discover and purchase.

- **Tea culture education:** Through Tea & Leaf Works by JDX, the company offers structured tea appreciation programs for businesses and children, building long-term customer relationships beyond transactional sales.
