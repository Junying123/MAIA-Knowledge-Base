## Who CK Auto Parts Is

CK Auto Parts is a specialist automotive parts distributor based in Malaysia, operating as a B2B wholesale supplier to workshops, repair centres, and trade accounts across the country. The business does not sell retail — its customers are the foremen, workshop owners, and mechanics who need the right part, reliably and quickly, to keep their bays moving.

The company carries a broad inventory of remanufactured, recycled, OEM, and builder parts covering every major make and model of car and light truck. Their range spans engine components, driveline parts, transmissions, brakes, suspension, and consumables — a catalogue of tens of thousands of SKUs serving the full spectrum of vehicles on Malaysian roads, from Perodua Myvi to Volkswagen Golf to Proton Saga.

What makes CK Auto Parts operationally distinct is the nature of their customer base and how orders flow. Their wholesale clients — regular trade accounts who buy frequently and in volume — do not browse a website and add items to a cart. They WhatsApp. They know what they want, they expect a fast answer on availability and price, and they want the order confirmed and the paperwork out the door with minimal friction. Speed and accuracy are the two things CK Auto Parts is measured on, every single day.

The business is growing. The customer base is expanding. The volume of enquiries coming through WhatsApp is increasing. And the complexity of managing parts compatibility across hundreds of vehicle models, multiple warehouse locations, and a layered pricing structure for different customer tiers is outpacing the manual systems that have carried the business to this point. That is the gap MAIA is built to close.

## How CK Auto Parts Operates Today

The current operating model at CK Auto Parts works because of the people running it. Their salespeople know their customers, know their stock, and know their prices. But the systems behind those people are fragmented, manual, and entirely dependent on individual memory — which creates real operational risk as the business scales.

### The WhatsApp Order Problem

Every customer enquiry arrives through WhatsApp. A workshop foreman messages in asking whether a specific brake pad fits a 2012 Myvi 1.3 Auto, or whether there is stock for a particular transmission seal. The salesperson checks mentally, or calls the warehouse, or digs through a price list. If the part is available, they quote a price verbally in the chat. If the customer confirms, the salesperson manually creates a quotation or sales order — often in a separate system — and then sends the document back.

The problem is that this entire process lives in individual WhatsApp threads, spreadsheets, and the salesperson's own memory. There is no shared visibility into what orders are in flight. There is no system check preventing a part from being committed to two customers when only one unit remains. There is no automatic link between a WhatsApp confirmation and the warehouse picking list. Every step is a manual hand-off, and every hand-off is a point where something can fall through.

### Parts Compatibility: The Knowledge Gap

One of the most persistent and costly problems in CK Auto Parts' day-to-day is wrong-part orders. A customer orders a 1.5cc part for a 1.6cc vehicle. A workshop foreman references the car by its colloquial name — "big eye Myvi" — rather than the standard model designation, and the salesperson picks the wrong variant. The customer receives the part, finds it doesn't fit, and the whole transaction unravels into a return, a replacement, and a damaged relationship.

Today, there is no structured database that maps parts to compatible vehicle models. Compatibility knowledge lives with individual salespeople who have learned it over time. When a new salesperson joins, they start from zero. When a customer asks about an unfamiliar variant, the answer depends on who picks up the phone. This is not a staffing problem — it is a systems problem. The knowledge exists in the business. It is simply not captured anywhere that makes it reliably accessible.

The same challenge applies to vehicle naming. The Malaysian market refers to the same car by multiple names depending on the generation, the year, the engine size, the transmission, and regional slang. A Perodua Myvi made between 2005 and 2011 might be called "Gen 1 Myvi", "old Myvi", "Myvi 1.0", "Myvi 1.3 auto", or simply "the first Myvi". Without a master taxonomy that maps all of these aliases to a single standard vehicle code, every product search is an exercise in interpretation.

### Inventory Across Multiple Locations

CK Auto Parts operates inventory across multiple outlet locations. When a customer enquires about a part, the answer is not simply whether the part exists in the catalogue — it is whether it is physically available at the right location, and if not, whether a nearby branch can fulfil it and within what timeframe. Today, answering that question requires manual checking across locations, phone calls to the warehouse, and educated guesses about transfer timelines.

The result is a service experience that feels slower and less reliable than it actually is. Parts are available, but the availability is invisible. The business knows its stock, but that knowledge is trapped in a system that customers and frontline staff cannot query in real time during an active conversation.

### The Pricing Consistency Problem

CK Auto Parts serves different customer tiers with different pricing profiles. Regular wholesale accounts may have negotiated rates. New accounts are quoted at standard prices. Returning customers expect consistency — if they were quoted a certain price three months ago, a significantly different price today requires justification. Without a searchable history of past quotes and customer-specific pricing, salespeople either quote from memory, err conservatively, or spend time manually searching through old documents to find the precedent.

The pricing problem is not primarily about margin — CK Auto Parts' sales team is experienced and capable. The problem is the time cost of archaeology: digging through historical quotes to find the right number for a returning customer instead of simply looking it up. In a business where enquiry volume is high and customer patience for slow responses is low, every minute spent on internal price research is a minute not spent serving the next customer.

### The Order Accuracy Concern

As CK Auto Parts prepares to open its ordering system to direct B2B customer access, a new operational risk emerges. Customers placing orders themselves — without a salesperson in the loop — will make mistakes. They will order the wrong part. They will confuse variants. They will enter the wrong quantity. And because those orders, once confirmed, deduct inventory and generate paperwork, a wrong order is not just an inconvenience — it is a stock discrepancy, a fulfilment error, and a customer service problem rolled into one.

The management team is acutely aware of this. Their instinct is right: early-stage automation with no human check is a recipe for chaos. The question is not whether to have oversight, but how to make that oversight targeted, fast, and sustainable at scale — rather than a bottleneck that defeats the efficiency gain the system was meant to deliver.

## After MAIA: What Changes

MAIA does not change what CK Auto Parts sells or who it sells to. It changes how enquiries become orders, how orders become documents, and how the business sees itself operating in real time. The starting point is WhatsApp — because that is where the customers already are.

### From Enquiry to Order in a Managed Flow

When a B2B customer messages in on WhatsApp, MAIA's AI chatbot — integrated through Chatwoot, the backend chat management platform — receives the message and checks whether the sender is a registered contact. If they are not, the system replies with a clickable link directing them to the main CS number. No raw phone number exposed. No manual intervention needed. The boundary is set automatically.

For registered contacts, the chatbot engages. It asks for the car plate number or car model first — because without knowing the vehicle, no parts recommendation can be accurate. For returning customers whose vehicles are already in the system, MAIA skips the registration step entirely and goes straight to "what are you looking for today?" The conversation is short, guided, and purposeful — never more than two or three questions before the customer has an answer or is connected to a live agent who can help.

When a customer confirms they want to order, MAIA runs a live stock check against Xeersoft — CK Auto Parts' inventory system — and presents availability. If stock is confirmed, MAIA generates a draft quotation and sends it back to the customer via WhatsApp for review. Once confirmed, the quotation converts to a Sales Order, pushes to Xeersoft, and the inventory is deducted. The customer receives the Sales Order document on WhatsApp. The whole loop — from enquiry to confirmed order to document — closes within a single chat thread.

### Parts Compatibility Becomes Systematic

The knowledge that currently lives in individual salespeople's heads moves into a structured, AI-queryable knowledge base. Every part in CK Auto Parts' catalogue is mapped to the vehicle models it fits — filtered by generation, engine capacity, transmission type, and year range. Every vehicle model is registered in a master taxonomy that captures all the aliases and colloquial names the Malaysian market uses, resolving them to a single standard code.

When a customer asks about a part for their "old Myvi 1.3 auto", MAIA resolves that to the correct taxonomy code, queries the compatibility matrix, and surfaces only the parts that actually fit that vehicle. When a customer describes a symptom — oil leaking, squeaking brakes, hard to start — MAIA maps that to likely parts and asks a targeted follow-up question to narrow the recommendation. The AI does not guess. It retrieves from structured data that CK Auto Parts controls and maintains.

The practical effect is twofold. Wrong-part orders drop because compatibility is checked by the system before the customer commits. And new salespeople become immediately useful because the institutional knowledge of the business — which parts fit which cars, under what conditions, with what caveats — is no longer locked inside senior staff.

### The Approval Threshold Works as a Safety Net

CK Auto Parts' management concern about early-stage ordering errors is addressed through a configurable approval threshold, not a blanket manual review of every order. Orders below RM 1,000 flow through automatically. Orders above the threshold are held and a notification is sent to the designated approver via the Chatwoot app. One tap to confirm — the order proceeds to Xeersoft. One tap to reject — the salesperson is notified to review. The threshold is a setting, not a fixed rule. As confidence in the system builds, it can be raised or removed entirely without any code change.

This structure also protects against a specific failure mode: abandoned orders holding inventory indefinitely. Every unactioned Sales Order has a configurable timeout. If no action is taken within the defined window, the order auto-cancels, inventory is released, and the relevant parties are notified. In a business that operates on a fast-in-fast-out inventory model, this matters.

### Management Sees the Business in Real Time

With MAIA in place, the question "what is happening right now" has an answer that does not require calling anyone. Active orders, their status, which are pending approval, which have been fulfilled — all visible from a dashboard. Stock levels across locations. Customer order history. Parts enquiry patterns. The data that currently exists only in WhatsApp threads and spreadsheets becomes structured, queryable, and actionable.

## Feature Deep Dive

### 1. WhatsApp Chatbot via Chatwoot

The customer-facing layer of MAIA for CK Auto Parts runs through Chatwoot — an open-source chat management platform that sits between WhatsApp and MAIA's order engine. Chatwoot manages incoming conversations, routes them to the AI chatbot or live agents, and gives the operations team a unified backend view of every active chat thread.

The AI chatbot is the first point of contact for every registered customer. Its job is to gather the information needed to check stock and generate an order in the shortest, most natural conversation possible. It does not announce itself as a bot. It does not present menus. It does not ask five questions before it has useful information. It converses — in English primarily, with Mandarin and Malay support — and guides the customer toward a completed order with as little friction as possible.

When the AI cannot resolve a request — stock is unavailable, the customer wants to negotiate, the order exceeds the approval threshold — it hands off to a live CS agent. The customer sees no change in the conversation. The CS agent receives a push notification on the Chatwoot app and picks up where the AI left off. The resolution button in Chatwoot closes the case and returns control to the AI for future messages from that contact.

### 2. Customer and Vehicle Profile Registry

Every customer in MAIA has a profile linked to one or more registered vehicles. The profile stores the customer's contact details, customer type, preferred language, and order history. Each vehicle record stores the plate number as the primary lookup key, the standard model code resolved from CK Auto Parts' vehicle taxonomy, engine capacity, transmission type, and year of manufacture.

For returning customers, the plate number is all MAIA needs. It retrieves everything else automatically. For new customers, the registration flow is embedded in the first conversation — the chatbot collects the vehicle details as part of the natural enquiry flow, not as a separate onboarding step. The data is captured once and never asked for again.

This profile registry is also the foundation for accurate parts compatibility. Without knowing the exact vehicle, no recommendation from the knowledge base is reliable. The registry is what makes the knowledge base useful.

### 3. RAG-Powered Parts Knowledge Base

The knowledge base is the AI's brain for parts recommendations. It is not a general-purpose language model making educated guesses — it is a structured retrieval system that only surfaces information that CK Auto Parts has explicitly entered and verified. The AI retrieves before it generates, and it only generates responses based on what it retrieves.

The knowledge base has three interconnected layers. The Parts Master holds every SKU with its description, brand, part type, use case, symptoms it addresses, product photos, and pricing. The Vehicle-Parts Compatibility Matrix maps each part to the specific vehicle models it fits, filtered by engine capacity, transmission, and year range, with fitment confidence scores and technician-verified notes. The Symptom-to-Part Mapping allows the chatbot to handle diagnostic conversations — when a customer describes a problem rather than naming a specific part, the system maps the symptom to likely causes, asks a targeted follow-up question, and narrows to the most accurate recommendation.

Nothing in the knowledge base is generated by AI. All of it is entered, verified, and maintained by CK Auto Parts' team. The AI's role is retrieval and presentation — not invention. If a part is not in the knowledge base for a given vehicle, the AI says so and escalates to a human agent. It does not guess.

### 4. Vehicle Model Taxonomy

The taxonomy is the dictionary that translates how customers refer to their cars into how CK Auto Parts' system identifies them. A single standard vehicle code — for example, `PERODUA_MYVI_GEN1` — can have dozens of aliases attached to it: "Gen 1 Myvi", "old Myvi", "Myvi 2010", "Myvi 1.3 auto", and whatever other colloquial names the market uses. When a customer uses any of those terms, the system resolves to the standard code before querying compatibility.

The taxonomy is managed through an admin interface. New aliases can be added at any time without a code deployment. New vehicle models — as new cars enter the market or as CK Auto Parts expands their compatible range — can be added by the team. The taxonomy is a living document that CK Auto Parts owns and maintains, not a static list that requires developer intervention to update.

### 5. Order Management: Quotation to Sales Order

The order flow in MAIA follows a deliberate sequence designed around CK Auto Parts' concern for order accuracy. Stock is checked before the customer commits. A Quotation is generated first — not a Sales Order — so the customer can review items and pricing before anything is confirmed. Only after explicit confirmation does MAIA convert the quotation to a Sales Order and push it to Xeersoft for inventory deduction and document generation.

This sequence matters because a confirmed Sales Order in Xeersoft has downstream consequences: inventory is deducted, the item is no longer available to other customers, and the fulfilment process begins. Reversing a wrongly confirmed Sales Order is possible but disruptive. The quotation step creates a natural checkpoint that reduces errors without adding meaningful friction to the customer experience.

### 6. Approval Threshold and Notification

The approval logic is straightforward but consequential. Every Sales Order is evaluated against the configured threshold. Below the threshold, it auto-confirms and pushes to Xeersoft immediately. Above the threshold, MAIA holds the order and sends a structured notification to the designated approver via Chatwoot: customer name, items, quantities, total value. One confirmation tap proceeds the order. One rejection tap notifies the salesperson to review.

The threshold is not hardcoded. It is a configuration setting that management can adjust as the business evolves. Phase 1, where only internal salespeople are placing orders, can run with a high threshold — RM 1,000 — because the salesperson is already a trusted actor. Phase 2, where B2B customers are placing orders directly, may warrant a lower threshold or a different approval structure entirely. The architecture supports this without requiring a rebuild.

### 7. Multi-Outlet Inventory Visibility

Stock availability in MAIA is not a binary yes-or-no. When a customer enquires about a part, MAIA checks not just the primary outlet but all registered locations in Xeersoft. If the part is unavailable at the nearest branch but in stock at another, MAIA surfaces that information — which outlet has it, and the estimated delivery timeframe. The customer can then decide whether to wait for transfer or take an alternative.

For mixed orders — where some items are available and others are not — MAIA surfaces the split before the customer confirms, not after. The customer knows what they are getting and what is pending. If partial fulfilment is appropriate, MAIA can generate a Sales Order for the available items and flag the remainder for live agent follow-up. No surprises after the order is placed.

### 8. Xeersoft Integration

MAIA is not a standalone system — it is the customer-facing and order-management layer that feeds into Xeersoft, which remains CK Auto Parts' inventory and finance source of truth. When a Sales Order is confirmed in MAIA, it pushes to Xeersoft in real time: customer details, line items, quantities, pricing. Xeersoft deducts inventory, generates the Invoice and Delivery Order, and sends confirmation back to MAIA. The Sales Order document is then returned to the customer via WhatsApp.

The API integration between MAIA and Xeersoft is already technically developed. What remains is the configuration, testing, and validation of each data exchange to ensure accuracy across every transaction type: standard orders, partial orders, cancelled orders, and auto-cancelled orders after timeout.

## Scope Definition

### Phase 1 — In Scope

Phase 1 is internal-only. The WhatsApp chatbot and MAIA order flow are operated by CK Auto Parts' salespeople, not their customers. The salesman takes the customer enquiry, uses MAIA to check stock and generate the order, and sends the resulting document to the customer. The AI assists the salesman; it does not replace the customer interaction entirely.

### Phase 2 — Planned Extension

Phase 2 opens the same WhatsApp ordering flow to selected B2B wholesale customers directly — no salesperson as intermediary. The chatbot handles the full cycle from enquiry to confirmed order for registered trade accounts. The approval threshold will be reviewed and likely adjusted for direct customer access. Phase 2 begins only after Phase 1 has been operated, tested, and stabilised.

### Future State — Not in Current Scope

B2C (public retail) access is explicitly deferred. CK Auto Parts' management does not want pricing and product availability visible to the general public or to competitors at this stage. The in-store iPad use case — where walk-in customers can self-serve at a physical location — is architecturally supported by Phase 1's vehicle registry and knowledge base, but the interface and deployment for that use case is not part of the current build.

### Explicitly Out of Scope

Payment processing and point-of-sale functionality. Customer-facing e-commerce. Integration with any system other than Xeersoft and Chatwoot. Automated collections or finance workflow. AI-generated product descriptions or catalogue enrichment. Any feature that requires MAIA to make autonomous decisions without a human confirmation step.

## How to Know If It Is Working

MAIA's value for CK Auto Parts is measurable. These are the concrete indicators that the system is delivering what it was built to deliver — and the ones to watch if something is going wrong.

### Order Accuracy

The primary failure mode MAIA is designed to prevent is wrong-part orders. Success means the rate of orders that result in returns, replacements, or customer complaints due to incorrect parts declines measurably after launch. If the rate of mis-orders does not fall within the first 60 days of Phase 2, the vehicle taxonomy, alias resolution, and compatibility matrix need to be reviewed and enriched.

### Enquiry-to-Order Conversion Time

Today, the cycle from customer enquiry to confirmed Sales Order involves manual steps at every stage. MAIA should compress that cycle significantly for straightforward orders. A target benchmark: orders below the approval threshold, for parts that are in stock, should complete the full cycle — enquiry to SO in Xeersoft — within a single chat session. If orders are consistently stalling at the quotation stage or requiring live agent intervention for standard requests, the chatbot flow needs refinement.

### Approval Queue Health

The approval threshold exists to catch exceptions, not to create a queue. If a significant proportion of orders are landing in the approval queue — more than 15 to 20 percent — the threshold may be set too low, or the customer base placing orders may not yet be ready for self-service. Conversely, if no orders are hitting the queue, the threshold may be too high to provide meaningful oversight.

### Live Agent Escalation Rate

Some escalation to live agents is expected and healthy — complex requests, out-of-stock situations, price negotiations. But if more than a third of all conversations are escalating before reaching a quotation, the knowledge base is either incomplete, the vehicle taxonomy has gaps, or the chatbot's conversation flow is not guiding customers effectively. Track what categories of enquiries are escalating most frequently and use that data to prioritise knowledge base enrichment.

### Inventory Accuracy

The integration between MAIA and Xeersoft is a two-way dependency. If stock levels in MAIA's checks diverge from actual warehouse stock — orders are confirmed for parts that turn out to be unavailable — the Xeersoft placement data is either not updating in real time or the multi-outlet query logic has a gap. Inventory accuracy is the foundation of customer trust, and it needs to be validated rigorously in the testing phase before Phase 2 goes live.

### The Harder Metric: Customer Experience

All of the above are internal indicators. The external measure is simpler: do CK Auto Parts' B2B customers find the WhatsApp ordering experience faster and more reliable than what they had before? If the answer is yes — if workshops are placing orders without needing to call, if parts are arriving correctly the first time, if the process feels frictionless — then MAIA is working. If customers are bypassing the chatbot and calling the salespeople directly because the system is slower or less reliable than a phone call, something needs to change.

MAIA handles the mechanics of the transaction. The judgment, the relationships, and the commercial decisions remain with CK Auto Parts' team. That is not a limitation of the system — it is the design principle. The goal is not to replace what makes CK Auto Parts good at what they do. It is to give their operational reality a structure that can grow with them.
