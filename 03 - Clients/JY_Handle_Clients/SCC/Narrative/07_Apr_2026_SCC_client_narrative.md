# SCC Client Narrative

## 1. Who SCC is

SCC is an established B2B business operating across multiple commercial workflows, including machine sales, animal feed, and related customer servicing needs. Their environment is not low-volume or casual. Orders are meaningful, customer expectations are high, and coordination across sales, finance, warehouse, and management matters. Current order volume is around 200 sales orders a month, with much of the operational flow still running through WhatsApp, email, fax, pictures, and manual follow-up. 

They are not coming into this conversation looking to replace employees. Their stated goal is to save employee time, reduce repetitive work, and improve efficiency. That matters because it tells you how they will judge MAIA: not by how flashy it is, but by whether it reduces operational friction without disrupting the way the business really works.

---

## 2. What is happening today

SCC’s business is currently held together by people, not by clean process control.

The team is working, decisions are being made, quotations are going out, orders are being processed, and stock is moving. But too much of that depends on manual coordination, informal communication, and individuals knowing how to navigate exceptions. Pricing is one example. Prices are dynamic, customer-specific, and sometimes given directly by the purchasing manager to the sales agent without being updated into SAP. Some of this is maintained in Excel, and some of it appears to exist only when someone asks for it. Quotations are valid for two weeks, which adds more time sensitivity.

Stock visibility is another example. SCC does not only need to know how much stock exists. They need to know how much stock is already reserved, who is holding it, which sales order it is tied to, which salesman requested it, and for which customer. Right now, that visibility is not clear enough, which means the team can commit stock without fully understanding what has already been informally locked up by someone else.

The same pattern exists in finance. SCC wants daily sales reporting on WhatsApp, SOA submission and extraction, knock-off support, invoice tagging, exportable summaries, and audit trail. That tells you finance visibility today is too fragmented and too manual.

The field sales side is similar. Salespeople are expected to report what happened after customer meetings, often through voice notes or informal updates. Management wants that compiled into a usable sales report, but the format is not yet even fully defined. That means the business currently generates field intelligence, but does not consistently turn it into structured, retrievable information.

Even the warehouse flow shows this pattern. Pick and pack lists currently come from SAP, then the team plans what to pack, how much to pack, and how many lorries to assign the next day. SCC is already thinking about grouping delivery orders by region, which shows they are not just concerned with document creation. They are concerned with how upstream decisions affect downstream execution.

---

## 3. What they are concerned about

SCC’s concerns are not random feature questions. They are trust questions.

They are worried about pricing because MAIA cannot simply show a number and assume that number is correct. Pricing in SCC is dynamic, negotiated, and partially outside the system of record. If MAIA gives the wrong price, it will not just create an admin issue. It will create commercial damage.

They are worried about stock reservation because stock is not just stock. It is a shared commercial resource that different salespeople may be competing for. Without visibility into who is holding what and for how long, MAIA could create false confidence and worsen internal friction instead of resolving it.

They are worried about SAP integration because they do not want a side tool. Salesmen may create quotations, but sales support or managers may need to approve, and the final output still has to go into SAP. They also raised the need for a role permission matrix, which means authority and access control matter to them from the beginning.

They are worried about overdue exposure because one company may be served across multiple departments, and outstanding amounts should be seen at the whole-company level, not in disconnected fragments. Their concern is that decisions will be made based on partial visibility unless the system reflects the true exposure.

They are worried about adoption because they know their business does not run on neat textbook workflows. It runs on real exceptions, real people, and real judgment. MAIA has to fit that reality. It cannot force the team into a simplified process that only works in demos.

They are also worried about continuity. When MAIA goes live, data can sync from SAP, but SCC already pointed out that some older invoices and chopped delivery orders live outside SAP in manual folders. That means they are already thinking about the gap between the new system and the operational memory of the old business.

---

## 4. What MAIA must prove to win their confidence

MAIA has to prove first that it understands SCC’s operating reality.

It must prove that it can support dynamic pricing without pretending SCC has a clean master price when it does not. It must prove that it can handle approval layers, not just create documents. It must prove that integration with SAP is real, not cosmetic. It must prove that reserved stock is visible in a way that helps salespeople negotiate and reallocate properly. It must prove that finance workflows become cleaner, not just digitized. And it must prove that field sales updates can become structured without forcing outdoor staff into unnatural admin behavior.

More bluntly, MAIA must prove that it can handle SCC’s exceptions, not just its happy path.

SCC will not trust MAIA because it can generate a quotation in chat. They will trust it when it can answer the harder questions:  
Who approved this?  
Is this really the right price for this customer today?  
Who is holding this stock?  
Can I release part of that reservation?  
What is the company-level overdue for this account?  
What happened in the last customer meeting?  
Can I still retrieve the older document the customer is asking for?

That is the threshold.

---

## 5. The narrative in one paragraph

SCC is an established B2B company trying to reduce the hidden cost of running sales, finance, and operational workflows through manual coordination. Their concern is not whether MAIA can automate a simple task. Their concern is whether MAIA can operate credibly inside a business where pricing is dynamic, approvals matter, stock reservation is politically sensitive, overdue exposure must be seen clearly, field updates are informal, and older documents do not always live in the main system. They will only gain confidence if MAIA proves it can support the real operating logic of SCC, especially where exceptions and judgment are involved.
